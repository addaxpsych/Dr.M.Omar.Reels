01

Respect the red zone

◆ No caption, no text, no important element may land in the red bands — on any layer, at any time. Instagram covers those edges with its own interface.

Measure the bounding box of the text, not the baseline — a descender or a second line sits lower than you think.

  • Put a guide layer at all four boundaries and keep it on while you work.
  • Check the widest caption in the episode, not the first one.
  • A background box counts as part of the element — its edges must stay inside too.
✳ One deliberate exception: the header (rule 08) sits above the 250 px line. That is intentional and matches the approved episodes — do not move it down to "fix" it.
02

No spelling mistakes — especially الهمزات and التاء المربوطة

This is the part the client notices. A single wrong letter undoes a well-built episode. Pay particular attention to the hamza forms (أ / إ / ا / آ) and to ة versus ه at the end of a word — by far the most common error.

Words that come up again and again, spelled correctly:

مهمة حاجة أسئلة أيوة حدة النتيجة
▲ Write the spoken words exactly as spoken. The doctor does not always say what the script says — what he said is what goes on screen. Never retype from memory, from the script, or from what you think you heard.

Use these sources, and double-check from your end:

  • Accurate SRT files — Series 2 — the SRT is the only source of on-screen text

04

Text inside a box must be perfectly centred — on all four sides

◆ Centre the box on the text's measured bounding box — horizontally AND vertically. Do not rely on centre justification, and do not centre on the text layer's anchor point.

With mixed Arabic and Latin text — which happens constantly here: medical terms, numbers, "IOL", "EDOF" — the text layer's origin sits at the left edge of the text, not its centre. Centre the box on the anchor and every pill in a stack drifts right by half its own width, each one by a different amount because they are different lengths. It looks like a mistake, because it is one.

Measure the rendered text rectangle, and place both the text and the box on that rectangle's centre. Then Position is the visual centre and everything lines up. Vertically, centre on the text's optical box, not the baseline — Arabic ascenders and descenders will otherwise push the text high in its pill.

05

Never use real surgical footage

◆ No exceptions. Not as b-roll, not blurred, not for a fraction of a second. Use the motion graphics and illustrated b-roll instead — see rule 06.

The footage behind it stays full-frame and dims for the length of the window: opacity 100% → 45% → 100%, Gaussian blur 0 → 98 → 0. Ramp in and out — do not hard-cut the dim.

Pulling the side camera back leaves a transparent strip across the top of the frame. Fill it with the background plate. Do not mask it, and do not scale the footage up to cover it — that is exactly what causes the pixelation in rule 13.

Every even line is orange. Line 1 white, line 2 #FFA806, line 3 white. On a 2-line caption that is the second line; on a 3-line caption the middle line. This is the signature caption look — do not skip it.

Drop shadow (the effect, not a layer style): black, 75% opacity, 210°, 5 px distance, 5 px softness. Do not replace it with a stroke — a stroke thickens the Arabic letterforms and reads worse.

13

Avoid pixelation when masking or removing a background

◆ Never scale footage up to cover a gap. Fill the gap with the background plate underneath instead — it costs nothing in quality. Scaling up to hide a transparent strip is the single most common cause of pixelation in these episodes.

The source footage is 1920 × 1080 landscape. At 1080 × 1920 you have to scale it to 177.78% to fill the frame — that is already an upscale, and it softens every shot. Before you start, either pre-upscale the source to 3840 × 2160 (the preferred fix — Series 1 already works this way), or build the comp at 608 × 1080 so the footage sits at 100% native pixels and upscale once on export.

When you key or mask, keep edges clean: do not feather away detail, and do not re-scale a keyed layer after the key. Any softness you introduce before the final upscale gets multiplied by it.
